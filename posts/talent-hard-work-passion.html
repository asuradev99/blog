I happen to disagree with this philosophy, but younger me wrote it so I want to keep track of it.

The distinction is often made between the hard worker and the talented individual. Comparisons such as "work beats talent when talent doesn't work" create a sense of competition between those who are innately gifted and those who work hard. I think it is certainly true, to some extent, that one must possess at least a bit of both of these qualities, especially hard work, to be successful in their chosen pursuit. But I think that there is perhaps another quality which is far more important than either of them. This is passion. It is the person who loves the thing they do, who does it not to prove that they are the best, or they work the hardest; not to dazzle others by the breadth and depth of their accomplishment, but merely because. Because the thing under study is so fascinating that one can become lost in it without further motive.

Talent without passion is initially impressive, yes, but peters out eventually when the gifted individual realizes that they no longer want to pursue it. Hard work without passion is akin to working for the sake of working, and will lead to a similar burnout to the first scenario. It is only those with a deep, persevering passion for what they do who will overcome any challenge to their study or setback to their progress, despite not being the hardest workers or the most talented people.

I would like to hope that I am of this third kind in my pursuit of physics, mathematics, and computer science. If I must settle on one of the three, it would be, without question, the third.
