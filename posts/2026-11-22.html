Had an important conversation with my parents today about my feelings of loneliness. We realized that when I say I am "bored" of a conversation, it may not necessarily be that I am bored, just that my anxiety of the conversation is preventing me from deriving some feeling of reward from the conversation, so I have to do extra work to be interested, making it extremely tiring. For certain subjects that I am less familiar with, like economics or politics, which are often more frequent subjects of conversations, this is especially the case. So maybe "bored" isn't the right word: tiring is.

Another very important realization: having a friend, someone who I am already familiar with, basically removes my social anxiety. I can sit down at a new table with that person's friend group and engage fairly normally, unimpeded by anxiety. The evidence for this is that last semester I had a friend, my lab partner in experimental physics, who I talked to a lot. Sometimes at dinner we would sit down and some of the people who knew him would join us, and I was able to discuss with them adequately. So this is one way that anxiety greatly reduces.
