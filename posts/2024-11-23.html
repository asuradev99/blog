2024-11-23

This week I took my final midterms (not final exams) for 6.1200 and 18.06. They went alright, though I think I made several careless mistakes on my 6.1200 exam which will add up to have a non-negligible impact on my score. Overall though, I'm not super worried.

After taking my linear algebra exam, I headed to the library again. Walking through the physical chemistry section, I had another fleeting moments of ecstasy when I am struck by the vastness of human knowledge. I could dedicate my entire life to the study of any one of these particular fields and not come close to knowing the entirety of it. Each discipline is like an infinite labyrinth, twisting to and fro in all directions. One can go any which way and never come back to where they started, for better or for worse.

I am beginning to realize that there is not enough time to be a perfectionist. There is not enough time to be a physicist, computer scientist, programmer, artist, musician, writer, worldbuilder, friend, son, father, teacher, and student all at the same time. With every passing day, I religiously gain expertise in some of these areas at the expense of others. The worlds, the creatures, the civilizations, those alien landscapes I see behind my mind's eye are slowly fading away, and the music repeats itself endlessly, trapped in the cage of never-being.

I have always wanted to be the perfect version of all these kinds of people: the most curious learner, the smartest physicist, the quickest programmer, the best son, the most compassionate teacher, etc. But life grinds away at you and wears you down. For the past six years of my life, I have been a good rat in the academic race, diligently following my studies at the expense of everything that isn't relevant or useful to this goal. I have suppressed my artistic side: no more compositions, no more carefree drawing, and progress at a snail's pace in terms of playing the piano.

Is the single-minded pursuit of academia worth it? In the depths of my heart I know the answer is no. But I am infected by a virus of workaholism: if only I can work a little harder, a little faster, a little smarter, I will unlock the secrets to the universe I have always dreamed of uncovering. I tell myself little stories: if I don't get an A, I'm done. If I'm not at the top of my class, I'm a failure. If I don't work twice as hard as other people, I'm not smart or talented enough to keep up. And, perhaps most significant of all, there is the voice inside of me that tells me that pursuing art is selfish -- it actively takes away time that I could be using to design better ways to help other people.

I wish I could say I know these feelings are false, but I don't know if they are. Perhaps the only reason I've survived as long as I have is purely on hard work and some amount of passion. It might be true that I'm not smart enough, and I must make up for it by working all day, 24-7. It might be true that slacking even a little in my grades means I am no longer fit for a career in theoretical physics, and that every second I spend on something else will not help at all. Finally, it might even be true that my art will reach no one and help no one.

But I also know that, 10 years from now, I would never forgive myself if I killed the artist to save the physicist. All those blissful memories - times of unhindered creativity and soul searching -- faded away. Those shapes, dreams, and worlds that I see are a core part of my identity - I have realized that much. Without them, I will be a shell of myself.

When I do try to create -- such as drawing or composing -- my motivation is always infected by the virus of perfectionism. This virus -- that everything must be perfect -- has worked incredibly well in academia. It has made me scrutinize every detail of everything that I am doing to make sure it is perfectly fine-tuned. But it has also killed a lot of my attempts to venture into the squishy realm of the subjective -- I quickly lose motivation to write or finish a composition because I get it into my head that no, my approach to this drawing or piece of music is fundamentally flawed and I need to start over. It is this very same attitude that has helped me in academia which has completely blocked progress in my artistic pursuits.

The little spark that started my journey into the internal wilderness that is my mind is fading. Deep down, I know I am fundamentally an artist, or at least that is how I started. But I will be an artist only in spirit if my path continues like this. I want the worlds inside my head to be free! I want to communicate, to share them with other people, to express the deepest form of myself which I have been unable to for my entire life. I want to bring back the artist.

Here is what I must do. I must draw to draw. I must compose to compose -- write to write. It is not the final, polished, perfect end result that matters. It is the process - the endless trial and failure, the gritty attempts to realize the songs and dreams in my head into reality -- that matters.

From 1000 failures I will improve. Little by little, I will bring a piece of the infinite mind-world into fruition. There is no time to wait -- I must start now.
