I don't believe in God.

Most of the time, I am something between agnostic and atheistic, with a naturalistic worldview. My reasoning goes as follows: there is not sufficient evidence to show that God (from any of the various major religions I am familiar with) actually exists, and many aspects of these proposed Gods are simply inherently untestable -- there will never be sufficient evidence. Additionally, I will only believe in something if I think there is sufficient evidence that it is true. Therefore, I do not believe in God.

However, I can relate to what I think are many of the main desirable aspects of religion. Long ago, our ancestors tried to make sense of a harsh and turbulent world, full of violent storms, freezing winters, ferocious predators, vengeful enemies, harrowing terrain, and deadly diseases. To cope with these uncertainties of life, our ancestors formed belief in higher powers as a means of social cohesion, humanizing the unkown, and enforcing morals.

Today, I think religion stems a little bit less from the desire for protection against the natural world, and is more related to being a part of a community. Religion makes the bond between families, friends, neighbors, etc. stronger -- a shield against the outside world.

And besides these aspects, I also think religion had something to do with our awe of the world. The world is not simply dangerous; it's different, teeming with strange, bizarre, and beautiful creatures, scenery, and situations which we hadn't encountered before. Giving religion credit for these vast variety of experiences makes us feel a little bit more in control of, or at least comfortable with, them.

This brings me to the one exception to my atheistic/agnostic beliefs. Sometimes, when my mind wanders away from the present reality, I sometimes find myself daydreaming of a kind of natural pantheism, similar to Spinoza's God https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Spinoza#Pantheism. Sometimes, when I look at a beautiful vista on a hike, or a serene river, or a sky full of stars, or even a particularly beautiful equation that comes up in my studies, I am filled with a great sense of awe for the world. Sometimes, very briefly, I am filled with awe for even the most mundane unnatural of things -- a simple pencil, for example, is made of materials and elements from all across the world, including a material, graphene, a two-dimensional material which is revolutionizing physics as we speak. The computer that I am typing this blog entry on is made of billions of tiny little devices which can somehow harness the power of light and electricity to create incredibly complex virtual patterns to appear on a screen, that can communicate with other such devices, nearly instantaneously, thousands of kilometers away invisibly, through electromagnetic waves. Even though we are used to these mundane items, if that is not a little bit awe-inspiring, I don't know what is. And not so dissimilar is us, with our human body, containing billions (trillions?) of cells, neurons, molecules, etc that are somehow simultaneously coordinating their electrical signals in such a way that makes us who we are and do what we do.

There are countless examples of facts like this that inspire me about the world. Sometimes, I cannot help but feel a sense that there is something far greater, vaster than I will every know in the universe -- a great cosmic web of mechanisms that is playing out everywhere like a grand play for all to see. In short, sometimes I cannot help but feel there is a God.

But this God is not a personal one. It is mindless, reasonless, emotionless, purposeless, amoral (at least, doesn't have one that I will ever know), feelingless -- completely devoid of anthropocentric notions. But the best part, perhaps the most important part about it is that I am it. Well, to be precise, I am a little part of God, just as everyone and everything is. The computer that sits on my desk is God. The electrons in my brain are God. In terms of anthropocentric concepts, it's the other way around: reason, morals, purpose, etc. are all a part of God. God is not enacting it's will over the universe and us -- God is the universe, which we are a little part of.

Absolutely everything, from the most dead, inanimate object you can think of to the most intelligent, alive being you can possible fathom, is connected in that it is God. Nothing is exempt; there is no mind that transcends physical reality -- no dualism; no hidden underlying layer of consciousness (Brahman); no overarching force of order (Tao). There is only the great, vast, universe. There is only us.

I really want to emphasize this point. When I say us, I don't just mean us human beings. We like to put ourselves in a category above everything else. We are living, conscious, rational beings, possessing of things like a mind, a soul, or qualia, we like to say. We are capable of complex, nuanced thought, and somehow this elevates us to a metphysical position which transcends even that of material reality. But in doing so, we lose track of where we come from. I might agree with have certain special capabilities, but this in no means we are somehow outside the more basic or fundamental forces that govern everything. We are simply rearrangements of the same components that everything else is made out of -- protons, neutrons, electrons, atoms, molecules, etc.

I think this is incredibly beautiful. Fundamentally, I am made of the same stuff as a rock. And yet, from this simplicity emerges a vast array of emergent complexity. Different chemicals, materials, different organisms, different you and me. As the physicist Philip Anderson coined, "More is different". In one view, I can zoom in and focus on all the incredibly intricate, special ways we are different. And in another, I when I zoom out, I only see one, unified thing, tangled together in a nest of cause and effect. Ultimately, there is no boundary between the self and the other, the inner and external world, us and the universe. It is all the same thing. Perhaps it is fitting to call this thing God.

But why do I use the word "God" when evidently this God is very much unlike the more conventional Gods of today's religions? I don't really know for sure. I do think that despite disagreeing with most religions as to the precise nature of a God, if it were to exist, I do share the very same sense of awe, wonder, reverence, respect that the religious speak of when talking about God. Ultimately, it's this deep emotion that sometimes, late at night, makes me feel just a little (ok, maybe more than a little) pantheistic.

Maybe I do believe in a God.
